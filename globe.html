Moon and Mars siting

Where would a city go?

New York grew where a sheltered harbor, rivers into the interior, fresh water and an island site all met in one place. Umbra asks the same question of NASA orbiter maps: where do the things a base needs sit within a short drive of each other? Click a site to see it on the globe.

Moon: the port cities

Near-side maria have the long flat runs a mass driver needs, ilmenite to make oxygen, and Earth overhead. Most functions in one place: Marius Hills, where a lava tube skylight sits on titanium-rich plains.

Moon: the water town at the south pole

Only one-kilometre blocks near the pole have all four: sun most of the year, ice within 2 km, ground flat enough to land and a pad site 2 to 5 km out. The best ones land on Shackleton's rim, beside the hubs our separate light-flow run found, and near Malapert and Mons Mouton, which are Artemis III candidate regions.

Mars

On Mars, water and year-round sun rarely meet. Shallow ice sits at mid latitudes; steady sun is at the equator. The best all-round hubs are low equatorial plains where Odyssey still sees a strong hydrogen signal.
