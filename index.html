Northern Chad beats every classic analog site.

Physics alone points to the Artemis landing areas.

We let 12,000 rover trips find their own routes over 72,000 km² of LRO LOLA terrain, chasing sun and avoiding slopes. Five of the twelve busiest hubs sit by Shackleton or de Gerlache. The routes cover 2% of the ground and get 1.6x the sunlight.

NASA's meteorite finds back up the "record survives" test.

Of 31,085 meteorite finds in NASA's catalogue, 67% fall in the quarter of Earth Umbra rates best for keeping a surface record intact. Random would give 25%. The test was built without the meteorites.

Ideas worth stealing

New ways to look for a Moon or Mars on Earth.

The challenge asked for places. Along the way we found angles that change what counts as an analog.

≈

Match the physics number

Two places behave alike when the number that drives the process matches, like the wind needed to lift a grain or the frost point of ice. Looks don't decide it.

⧗

Time is a test

The Moon keeps records for billions of years. So the question becomes how fast Earth erases them, and where it barely does.

$

Underpriced analogs

Value a place by how many jobs it can do, and price it by how much attention it already gets. High value, low price: go there next.

↝

Let physics route traffic

Simulate thousands of rover trips and see where the paths pile up. The hubs are where a base wants to be.

Hidden channels in NASA Moon data

We went through 21 kinds of NASA Moon data. Most measure more than they are known for: the laser altimeter also reads roughness and frost, and the thermometer also reads rock size and soil packing. Six of them have the same method already flying over Earth, so their Earth twins can be tested here.

ICESat-2 · GEDINISARASTER · ECOSTRESSEMITGRACE-FOAirborne gamma
⌂

Systems are analogs too

Earth already runs sealed life support: submarines balance their air for months, potable-reuse plants close the water loop, and SNOLAB runs a clean lab 2 km down a working mine. Those are rehearsals for a Moon base, not just places.

≠

Say what Earth can't copy

Every test carries an honest penalty for what will not carry over, like one-sixth gravity or hard vacuum. The list of gaps is a result in itself: it says what has to be tested in space.

How it works

Start from what NASA measured, not what a place looks like.

Red rock and grey dust make good photos. A drill, a seal or a battery cares about cold, dust, pressure and darkness. So Umbra starts with the numbers.

Measure

Each test begins with a real NASA reading of the Moon or Mars: how cold the polar craters get, how steep and dark the ground is, how thin the air is on Mars.

LRO DivinerLRO LOLAMGS MOLAMGS TESOdyssey GRS

Score Earth

Every land cell gets 0 to 100 per test. Is the condition there? Does it last? And how much of the real thing can Earth not copy?

MODIS TerraNASA POWERMERRA-2NASA GIBS

Bundle into trips

Where many tests land in Earth's top 3% inside one 2° block, a single expedition can rehearse many systems at once. Those blocks become the trips.
